**28. What could explain her oligohydramnios?**
Most likely **uteroplacental insufficiency (± growth restriction)** — supported by decreased FM, non-reactive NST, AMA, fibroid uterus, and an aneuploid/anomalous fetus; reduced renal perfusion lowers fetal urine output. Also consider/exclude:
- The fetal aneuploidy and anomalies themselves (T21 with AVSD; short femur may reflect FGR).
- **Undiagnosed PROM** — no speculum/nitrazine/AmniSure documented; must be excluded.
- Fetal renal/urinary tract anomaly (usually earlier onset; not described on the second-trimester scan).
- Cardiac dysfunction from the AV canal defect (more often causes hydrops/polyhydramnios).
- Maternal: dehydration; hypertension (BP normal → unlikely); diabetes/vascular disease (not documented); NSAID/ACE-I exposure (none).
- Placental pathology (infarcts/abruption), possibly fibroid-related; idiopathic.
*Confidence: Medium*

**30. Why minimal variability without accelerations?**
- **Fetal hypoxemia/acidemia from placental insufficiency** — most concerning and most consistent with decreased FM + AFI 3 cm; chronic hypoxia depresses CNS/autonomic modulation, abolishing variability and accelerations before decelerations appear.
- Fetal quiet-sleep cycle (20–40 min, occasionally longer) — benign; requires extended tracing (40–120 min) or vibroacoustic stimulation.
- Fetal CNS/cardiac abnormality — T21 and AVSD can blunt FHR reactivity; conduction defect possible.
- Prematurity — minor at 34 wk (≥32-wk criteria apply).
- Maternal sedatives, magnesium, steroids, smoking, hypoglycemia — none apply (PNV only, never smoker).
In context this should be treated as possible fetal compromise until proven otherwise.
*Confidence: Medium*
